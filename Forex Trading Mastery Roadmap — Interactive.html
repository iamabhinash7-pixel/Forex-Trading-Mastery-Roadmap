Step 1 — Beginner

Forex Foundations

How the market works, currencies, pips, and lots

What is Forex?

The Foreign Exchange market is where currencies are bought and sold — $7.5 trillion traded every single day, making it the largest financial market on earth. It runs 24 hours a day, 5 days a week, across four major sessions: Sydney → Tokyo → London → New York. You always trade in pairs: buying one currency while simultaneously selling another.

Support & Resistance

Support = price "floor" where buying historically stops declines. Resistance = price "ceiling" where selling historically stops advances. A level is only valid if price has touched it at least twice. When resistance is broken, it often becomes new support — this "role reversal" is one of the most reliable patterns in all of forex.

🚨 Biggest Beginner Mistake: Seeing patterns everywhere (confirmation bias). One touch is NOT a support level — it's just a price. Require at least 2 clear bounces. Always zoom out to Daily before looking at 5-minute charts.

The Four Essential Indicators

What it measures

Trend direction. The 200 EMA is the most important line on any chart. Price above it = bullish. Price below it = bearish. The 20/50 EMA crossover gives entry signals.

Key signals

Golden cross Fast EMA crosses above slow = buy signal.

Death cross Fast EMA crosses below slow = sell signal.

Pro tip: NEVER trade against the 200 EMA. If price is below it, only look for short setups. If above, only longs.

What it measures

Momentum. Oscillates between 0–100. Above 70 = overbought (buyers exhausted). Below 30 = oversold (sellers exhausted). Best setting: RSI(14).

Advanced: Divergence

Price makes new HIGH but RSI makes LOWER HIGH → momentum weakening → reversal likely. One of the most reliable signals in all of technical analysis.

Warning: In a strong trend, RSI can stay above 70 (or below 30) for a LONG time. Don't sell just because RSI is 72. Confirm with trend direction first.

What it measures

Trend + momentum combined. Built from 12/26 EMA difference, plotted with a 9-period signal line. The histogram shows the gap between them — watch for histogram bars shrinking to zero.

Key signals

MACD line crosses above signal line → bullish momentum building.

Histogram bars flip from negative to positive → momentum has shifted.

Pro tip: Use MACD as CONFIRMATION, not a standalone signal. It lags — by the time it crosses, price may have already moved significantly.

What it measures

Volatility. Three lines: 20-period SMA in the middle, two bands set 2 standard deviations above and below. Bands expand in high volatility, contract in low volatility.

The Squeeze

When bands narrow tightly = low volatility consolidation about to end. An explosive breakout is coming. Use another indicator to confirm the direction of the breakout.

Pro tip: Price can "walk the band" in a strong trend — touching the upper band repeatedly without reversing. Band touch alone is NOT a sell signal.

Step 4 — Intermediate

Building a Trading Strategy

Entry/exit rules, timeframes, backtesting

A strategy means written rules for every decision before you open a chart. No improvising. No gut feelings. A 45% win-rate strategy with 1:3 R:R prints money. A prediction approach with no rules loses money even when you're right more than wrong.

The EMA Pullback Strategy — Your Complete Playbook
Beginner–Intermediate EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY · 4H + 1H Timeframes

Setup Requirements

ParameterRule
PairsEUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY — major pairs only (tightest spreads)
Timeframe4H chart for setup identification · 1H for entry refinement
SessionLondon (8am–12pm GMT) or New York (1pm–5pm GMT)
AvoidFriday after 5pm GMT · NFP · CPI · Central bank rate decisions

Entry Checklist — ALL Must Be True

  • Price is ABOVE the 200 EMA on the 4H chartBullish bias confirmed — only take long (buy) trades
  • The 200 EMA is visibly sloping UPWARD (not flat)Flat EMA = no trend = no trade
  • Price has pulled back at least 30 pips from a recent swing highToward the EMA zone — this is your entry window
  • RSI(14) dipped BELOW 50 during the pullback (ideally 40–45)Sellers losing momentum — setup is almost ready
  • RSI is now TURNING BACK UPCurrent RSI value is higher than the previous candle's RSI
  • A BULLISH CONFIRMATION candle closes near EMA zoneEngulfing, hammer, or strong green body — this is your trigger
Tick all 6 conditions above. If any is missing — no trade.

Backtesting — The Step Everyone Skips

Before trading on demo, scroll back 3–6 months on EUR/USD 4H and manually test your rules. Use TradingView's free Replay Mode (clock button in toolbar). Minimum 30 historical trades.

  • Use TradingView Replay Mode (free)Press the replay/clock button in the top toolbar — advance candle by candle
  • Log 30+ historical trades in a spreadsheetDate, entry, SL, TP, result in R, was the setup valid?
  • Calculate win rate and expectancy(Win Rate × Avg Win) − (Loss Rate × Avg Loss) must be positive
  • Only proceed to demo trading after positive backtest resultsThis is your proof of edge — don't skip it
🚨 Biggest Beginner Mistake: Skipping backtesting and going straight to live trading. Every trader who blows their first account traded real money before truly knowing their strategy.

Trading Psychology

You are your own biggest enemy. Not the market, not your broker. YOU. You can have a backtested strategy, perfect position sizing, a complete playbook — and still lose money. Because the moment real emotions enter the picture, rational rules become very difficult to follow.

The Professional Mindset
BEGINNER THINKS

"Will this trade win?"

PROFESSIONAL THINKS

"If I execute this correctly 50 times, the statistics will produce profit."

A loss on a valid setup is NOT failure — it is one data point in a statistical series. You followed your rules. That IS success. A win on an invalid setup is dangerous — it rewards rule-breaking.

Step 7 — Master

Advanced Mastery

Fundamentals, macro analysis, intermarket, going live

Advanced traders don't choose between technical and fundamental analysis — they use both together. Fundamentals tell you the direction the river is flowing. Technicals tell you exactly where to step in. This is where all 7 steps converge.

Fundamental Analysis

Interest rates are the single most powerful fundamental driver of any currency pair. Money flows to where it earns the highest return. When a central bank raises rates, foreign capital pours in to earn better yields → currency appreciates. Always ask: which country has higher rates, and is that rising or falling?

ScenarioCurrency Impact
Country A raises ratesBullish Currency A strengthens vs all peers
Country A cuts ratesBearish Currency A weakens vs all peers
Rate hike expected (not yet announced)Bullish Markets price it in advance — buy the rumour
Hike disappoints (smaller than expected)Bearish "Sell the fact" — market was positioned for more

CPI (Consumer Price Index) measures inflation. Central banks have a target — usually 2%. When CPI is above target, the central bank is likely to raise rates to cool the economy → bullish for that currency. When CPI is below target, rate cuts may be needed → bearish. Watch the monthly CPI release — it's a market mover.

US Non-Farm Payrolls (NFP) is released the first Friday of every month at 8:30am EST. It is the most market-moving scheduled event in all of forex. Strong jobs data → healthy economy → Fed likely to keep rates high → USD bullish. Weak data → USD bearish. Do NOT trade in the 30 minutes before or after NFP until you're experienced.

Advanced Price Action Concepts
Order Blocks — Where Institutional Orders Live›
The last bullish or bearish candle before a significant price move away from a level. Institutions place large orders here. Price often returns to these zones for a second fill. Bullish order block = last down candle before a strong up move. Bearish order block = last up candle before a strong drop. These zones act as high-probability support/resistance.
Fair Value Gaps — Imbalances Markets Fill›
A three-candle pattern where the wicks of candle 1 and candle 3 don't overlap. Price moved so fast it left a "gap" in market structure — an area where buyers and sellers never traded fairly. Markets tend to return to fill these gaps before continuing. High-probability entry zones when aligned with trend direction.
Liquidity Sweeps — Where Stops Get Hunted›
Retail traders' stop losses cluster just below swing lows and above swing highs. Institutions deliberately push price through these levels to trigger those stops, creating liquidity they need to fill their own large orders, then immediately reverse. Watch for: a sharp spike through a key level followed by a strong reversal candle. Trade the reversal after the sweep is confirmed.
Market Structure Shifts — Earliest Reversal Signal›
In an uptrend (HH + HL), a market structure shift (MSS) occurs when price breaks below the most recent higher low with a strong candle. This is the earliest warning that the trend is changing — earlier than any moving average crossover. Gives you better entries at trend transitions.
Session Killzones — When Institutions Are Active›
Institutional activity clusters in specific time windows: London open (7–9am GMT), New York open (12–2pm GMT), and the London/NY overlap (12–4pm GMT). The Asian session (midnight–6am GMT) is typically low volatility and often sets up liquidity that London and NY then sweep. Target these killzone windows for your setups.
